            Questions about finding purpose

            How do I find my purpose in life?

            Researchers define purpose as a stable intention to accomplish something that is meaningful to you and that also contributes to the world beyond yourself (Damon, Menon & Bronk, 2003). To find yours: notice what activities energize you, name your strengths, choose a problem in the world you care about, write a one-sentence purpose statement, then test it with small real-world experiments for 30 days. Purpose is built through action more than discovered by thinking.

            What is ikigai?

            Ikigai (生き甲斐) is a Japanese word meaning "reason for being" or "what makes life worth living". Psychiatrist Mieko Kamiya wrote about it in 1966. Researchers measure it with the Ikigai-9 scale (Imai et al., 2012), which has been validated in English (Fido, Kotera & Asano, 2020).

            Is the ikigai Venn diagram a Japanese idea?

            No. The popular four-circle diagram (what you love, what you're good at, what the world needs, what you can be paid for) was published by Marc Winn in a 2014 blog post, adapting an earlier purpose diagram by Andrés Zuzunaga. In Japan, ikigai doesn't have to involve work or money. Pathfinder uses the diagram as a practical reflection tool, and uses the Ikigai-9 scale to measure ikigai itself.

            Why does having a purpose matter?

            In a study of about 6,000 U.S. adults followed for 14 years, people with a greater sense of purpose were less likely to die during follow-up, at every age (Hill & Turiano, 2014). A study of 6,985 adults over 50 found the same pattern (Alimujiang et al., 2019). These studies are correlational, so they show a strong link rather than proof that purpose causes longer life.

            What should I do when I feel lost in life?

            Feeling lost is a common starting point, not a failure. Start small: for one week, write down which moments give you energy and which drain you; talk to two people whose lives interest you; and try one low-risk experiment. If feeling lost comes with lasting sadness or hopelessness, talk to a doctor or counselor, or find a free helpline at findahelpline.com.

            Do I need to change my job to live my purpose?

            No. Purpose can be lived through volunteering, caregiving, mentoring, a side project, or "job crafting" — reshaping the tasks and relationships in your current role toward what matters to you (Wrzesniewski & Dutton, 2001).

            Is the Pathfinder test free and private?

            Yes. It's free, needs no sign-up, and your answers are stored only in your own browser. Nothing is sent to a server.
